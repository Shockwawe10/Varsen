I

Assembled the way a mechanic builds it

  1. Frame

    On the stand first. Every other part is set from it: the head tube, the dropouts, the bottom bracket.

  2. Fork

    Slides up into the head tube from below, ready to take the front wheel.

  3. Wheels

    Front and rear go in on their thru-axles, the rear with its cassette already fitted.

  4. Drivetrain

    The cranks go in through the bottom bracket, one arm from each side. The chain goes on last, once there is a ring and a cog to wrap.

  5. Cockpit

    Lowered onto the steerer and set square to the front wheel.

  6. Saddle

    Last: the post slides into the seat tube until the saddle sits 725 mm from the bottom bracket. Ready to ride.

II

In detail

The parts that make the R1, up close. Choose a point on the bike to look closer.

Frame and fork

Carbon, aero-profiled. The down tube deepens from 49 to 62 mm into the bottom bracket, the chainstays are 410 mm on every size, and the fork has 44.4 mm of offset. The geometry for your size is in the next chapter.

Drivetrain

52/36 chainrings on a four-arm spider and 170 mm cranks, into an 11–30 cassette. A 112-link chain runs over two 11-tooth jockey wheels.

Brakes

Flat-mount calipers on the side away from the chain, on rotors of 160 mm up front and 140 mm at the rear, each drilled with 30 holes on a six-arm carrier. The rear wheel runs on a 142 mm thru-axle through slotted dropouts.

Wheels

Rims with a hooked bead seat and a rounded spoke bed, on 26 mm tyres with a file tread on the shoulders. 24 bladed spokes a wheel: radial at the front, two-cross at the rear. The front turns on a 100 mm thru-axle.

Cockpit

The stem runs 100 mm at −6° into a 420 mm bar with flattened tops. The hoods sit on the forward bend, and the tape wraps the drops.
